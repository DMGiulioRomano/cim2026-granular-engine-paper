\begin{abstract}
Composing at the scale of the grain means governing a population of events, not
declaring them one by one. Existing declarative environments — tendency-mask
generators such as CMask, pattern libraries in SuperCollider — state outcomes
rather than procedures, but are not built to make the generated grain population
inspectable: composition stays confined to a write-listen-rewrite cycle. We
present DIRAC, an environment built around a YAML-based, versionable, reproducible
specification, materialized grain by grain by a deferred-time engine that records
every parameter of every grain. Two mechanisms extend the granular tradition's
control. A probability gate decouples the amplitude of a per-grain deviation from
the likelihood that it occurs, turning a fused parameter into two independent,
continuously modulable axes. A Multiparametric Audio Plot (MAP) renders the whole
specification as a synoptic image whose vertical axis is the reading position in
the source material, letting the composer see where each grain is drawn from
instead of inferring it by ear. The system proceeds from the single grain to
multi-voice texture through one deviation at a time. Its scope is bounded: it is
blind to the timbre of the granulated material, parameters are independent by
construction, and the MAP encodes pitch through color alone.
\end{abstract}
