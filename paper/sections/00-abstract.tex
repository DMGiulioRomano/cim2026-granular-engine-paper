\begin{abstract}
Granulation of sampled material places compositional control at the scale of the
grain, where the result emerges from populations of events rather than from
parameters traceable one-to-one. In real-time systems this control stays opaque,
confined to a non-inspectable DSP layer, opening a distance between what is declared
and what is heard. The granular tradition has consolidated robust models —
synchronous and asynchronous emission, tendency masks, microtemporal decorrelation.
Declarative sound languages, from score generators to pattern libraries, state
outcomes rather than procedures, yet the specification is compiled and consumed,
leaving no intermediate level at once queryable and synoptically readable. We present
DIRAC (Declarative Intermediate Representation for Audio Composition): the composer
declares the intended outcome in a YAML-based domain-specific language, translated
into a still-declarative, queryable IR and then materialized in deferred time, grain
by grain. A Multiparametric Audio Plot (MAP) returns the whole specification as a
synoptically inspectable image. The system proceeds by progressive deviations from
the identity transform, from the single grain to the multi-voice texture, making the
control of the granular mass thinkable and readable.
\end{abstract}
